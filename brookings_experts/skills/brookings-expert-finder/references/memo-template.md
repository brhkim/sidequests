# Memo structure

The reader is deciding who to email in the next ten minutes. Write for someone
who will read the top recommendation carefully, skim the rest, and want to
justify the choice to a colleague.

## Required structure

```markdown
# Brookings experts on <specific question, not broad subject>

**Topic:** <one sentence naming the actual question at issue>
**Read as:** <consult mode> — <the phrasing that led you there>
**Roster snapshot:** <build date from expert-index.md> · **Checked:** <today>

## Recommendations

### 1. <Name> — <primary title>
**Why them.** <2-4 sentences on fit with this specific question, not the general
subject. Name the angle they cover that the others do not.>
**Recent work.** <Dated, linked, most-relevant-first. 2-3 items.>
- [Title](url) — <date>
- [Title](url) — <date>
**Background.** <Training and prior roles that bear on this question. Only what
is relevant — a Treasury stint matters on a debt story and not on a schools one.>
**Access.** <Appointment type, seniority, realistic difficulty, routing note.>

### 2. <Name> — <primary title>
<same shape>

### 3. <Name> — <primary title>
<same shape>

## Why this order

<A short paragraph, not a restatement of the entries. Say what drove the
ranking — usually that the top name has current published work on the precise
question while others are strong on the broader subject. Name the tradeoff the
reader is making if they go down the list instead.>

## Also considered

<2-4 one-liners: name, why they were plausible, why they placed lower. This is
where quiet-but-expert and hard-to-book people belong, so the reader can
overrule you.>

## Gaps

<Angles no one on this list covers, and any candidate you could not verify.
Omit only if genuinely nothing is missing.>
```

## What separates a good memo from a keyword match

**Name the angle, not the field.** "Covers monetary policy" is useless — every
name under that tag covers monetary policy. "Has argued specifically that the
labor-market data the Fed is reacting to is being misread" tells the reader why
this person and not the other nine.

**Let the dates carry the argument.** If your top pick published on this three
weeks ago and the runner-up last wrote about it in 2023, the reader sees the
ranking rationale without your having to assert it.

**Be concrete about access.** "May be difficult to reach" says nothing. "Program
director — booking goes through Brookings comms and typically takes several days;
worth it if you want institutional weight behind the quote" is actionable.

**Write the gaps section honestly.** A reader who discovers the omission
themselves stops trusting the memo. A reader you warned about it trusts you more.

## Worked fragment

Illustrative only — do not reuse these claims. Note how the recency evidence does
the ranking work, and how the access note informs rather than discourages.

```markdown
### 1. Aaron Klein — Miriam K. Carliner Chair, Economic Studies; Senior Fellow, Center on Regulation and Markets

**Why them.** Klein works on payments plumbing specifically, which is the part of
this story most coverage is getting wrong — the dispute is about settlement
mechanics, not about crypto adoption. He has been arguing the regulatory-design
side of stablecoin implementation since the GENIUS Act passed, so he can speak to
what the rule actually changes rather than to the announcement.

**Recent work.**
- [Public comment to OCC on implementation of the GENIUS Act](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/public-comment-to-occ-on-implementation-of-the-genius-act/) — May 2026
- [Next steps for GENIUS payment stablecoins](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/) — 2026

**Background.** Former chief economist of the Senate Banking Committee and
Treasury deputy assistant secretary — the legislative-history angle here is
directly his.

**Access.** Resident senior fellow with a named chair, so in demand on any
payments story; he publishes and comments frequently, which suggests he is
reachable. Brookings Economic Studies media office is the route.
```
