# Memo: Brookings scholars on Sahel debt distress and the security nexus

**Date:** September 13, 2026
**Re:** Who to talk to before next week's program team briefing

---

## Bottom line up front

**No one at Brookings currently owns the Sahel debt-security nexus as a file.** The
institution is strong on two halves that do not quite meet: a continental/global
sovereign-debt bench (Songwe, Kharas, Mohieldin) that rarely writes about Mali,
Burkina Faso or Niger specifically, and an Africa Security Initiative that covers
coups and partner militaries but not fiscal mechanics. The Africa Growth Initiative
has no francophone Sahel macroeconomist on staff.

That is a real finding, not a search failure — plan on **three conversations plus
outside supplementation**, not one call.

**A framing caution before you brief.** "Restructuring talks" may be the wrong lens
for the central Sahel. Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger are WAEMU members with no
Eurobonds in play; they are not Common Framework cases like Zambia, Ghana or
Ethiopia. Their distress runs through the **regional CFA-franc bond market**:
Mali is shut out of external markets and operating under a no-money IMF reference
programme, with ~58% of public debt in CFA francs, 62% of maturities inside three
years, and domestic arrears around 2.5% of GDP (vs. 0.7% external). Niger has been
paying double-digit yields on short maturities and shrinking its debt stock because
it repays faster than it can refinance. The security interaction is therefore mostly
**domestic-bank balance sheets, arrears to local suppliers, and a security wage bill
crowding out everything else** — not creditor-committee diplomacy. Anyone who briefs
you on Common Framework mechanics is answering a different question. Worth deciding
which question your program team actually has.

## Tier 1: the three people to actually contact

### 1. Vera Songwe — the debt half
*Nonresident Senior Fellow, Global Economy and Development / Africa Growth Initiative*
https://www.brookings.edu/people/vera-songwe/

The strongest sovereign-debt voice with an Africa mandate anywhere in the building.
Former UN Under-Secretary-General and Executive Secretary of UNECA; chair of the
Liquidity and Sustainability Facility; ex-World Bank/IFC. Actively publishing on
this in 2026 — a February 2026 piece argued from the Ethiopian case that drawn-out
restructuring negotiations leave African sovereigns exposed to external shocks, and
in April 2026 she was writing on rising Treasury yields pushing up African
debt-service costs. Her stated 2026 theme is a framework that surfaces hidden debt
early and closes restructurings fast enough to deliver real relief.

- **Ask her:** why restructurings take so long and what the delay costs a borrower;
  whether the Common Framework has anything to offer a WAEMU sovereign; how hidden
  and domestic debt gets missed in DSAs.
- **Caveat:** her work is architecture-level and continental. She is Cameroonian and
  fluent in CFA-zone mechanics, which helps, but do not expect Bamako-specific
  detail, and do not expect the security analysis at all.

### 2. Alexander Noyes — the security half, and the closest thing to a live Sahel file
*Fellow, Foreign Policy and the Strobe Talbott Center; Africa Security Initiative*
https://www.brookings.edu/people/alexander-noyes/

Expertise in U.S. defense policy, military assistance, irregular warfare, **coups**
and democratization. He co-authored **"Why Mali is at a dangerous crossroads"
(June 11, 2026)** with Chidi Blyden — the most recent Sahel-specific item Brookings
has published. Two books out in 2026: *War at Arm's Length* (Yale, with Richard
Bennet) on building effective partner militaries, and *Compromised Coalitions*
(Penn) on post-conflict power sharing.

- **Ask him:** the trajectory of the Bamako blockade and JNIM pressure on supply
  lines; what the AES junta consolidation (joint force stood up December 2025,
  confederation treaty, AES investment bank) means for external engagement; how
  Western disengagement and Russian security partnerships change who is willing to
  lend or reschedule.
- **Note:** Chidi Blyden is his co-author, not a Brookings fellow — she is Deputy CEO
  of the Millennium Challenge Corporation and former DASD for African Affairs. If you
  want the U.S. development-finance angle on the same countries, she is a separate and
  very well-placed call, but you would be reaching her at MCC.

### 3. Ahmadou Aly Mbaye — the francophone West Africa bridge
*Nonresident Senior Fellow, Global Economy and Development / Africa Growth Initiative;
Vice-Chancellor, Université Cheikh Anta Diop, Dakar*
https://www.brookings.edu/people/ahmadou-aly-mbaye/

The only Brookings-affiliated economist based in the region with deep francophone
West African political economy. Best known for reframing the formal/informal
dichotomy across Francophone Africa; also works on regional integration and climate
economics, and coordinates programmes across ten African countries.

- **Ask him:** how the WAEMU regional bond market actually behaves under stress and
  who the marginal buyer is; what informality and a shrinking tax base do to the
  Sahel's fiscal ceiling; who in Dakar and Abidjan he would put you in front of.
- **Caveat:** debt and security are not his published subjects. Treat him as your
  regional-context and network call, not your debt technician.

## Where Brookings will not get you there

Be candid with the program team that the institution's Sahel output is thin and
episodic. The Mali landing page currently holds three items: the June 2026 Noyes/Blyden
piece, a December 2025 essay on Malian democratic consultations (Bleck and Soumano,
both external authors), and a 2023 piece on West African insecurity and growth
(Siaplay and Werker — also external, and pre-dating the AES entirely). *Foresight
Africa 2026* handles debt at the continental level, with Raymond Gilpin (UNDP, not
Brookings) writing on credit ratings; it has no Sahel debt chapter.

To close the gap, supplement with:

- **IMF** — the binding documents. Mali Country Report 25/255, Burkina Faso 26/207,
  the April 2026 Sub-Saharan Africa Regional Economic Outlook, and the WAEMU
  Article IV. These are where the arrears and maturity-bunching numbers above come
  from and they are more current than anything in the think-tank literature.
- **World Bank/IMF joint Debt Sustainability Analyses** for each of the three states.
- **International Crisis Group** and **ISS Africa** — the standing Sahel security
  benches, with people who travel there.
- **ACLED** for the violence data behind any trend claim.
- **Ecofin Agency** and WAEMU/UMOA-Titres auction data for the regional bond market
  plumbing — this is where the refinancing story is actually visible week to week.
- **Timbuktu Institute** (Dakar) for regional-analyst perspective.

## Suggested sequence

1. **Nguimkeu** (short, by email) to confirm there is no AGI Sahel person you are missing.
2. **Noyes** for the security trajectory — he has the most current material.
3. **Songwe** for the debt logic, framed explicitly as "what applies and what does not
   apply to a WAEMU sovereign with no Eurobonds."
4. **Mbaye** if you need regional texture or Dakar introductions.

Pull the IMF Mali and Burkina Faso reports before any of these calls; they will make
all four conversations sharper, and they carry the numbers your program team will ask for.
