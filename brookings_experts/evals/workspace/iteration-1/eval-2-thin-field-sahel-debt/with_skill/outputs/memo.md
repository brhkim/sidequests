# Brookings experts on how Sahel insecurity is shaping sovereign debt restructuring talks

**Topic:** How the security situation in the Sahel — jihadist blockades, coups, the AES break with ECOWAS — is feeding into fiscal distress and into the politics and pace of debt restructuring negotiations with the IMF and other creditors.
**Read as:** Decision briefing — "brief our program team," "help me get smart on this." Ranked for synthesis, breadth, and people who can sit with a team for an hour, not for a same-day quote.
**Roster snapshot:** 2026-09-13 · **Checked:** 2026-09-13

A note before the list. This is a genuinely thin field. Brookings has real strength
on African sovereign debt architecture and real strength on Sahel security, but
almost nobody who writes at the joint. One person on this list works the actual
intersection; the rest are one half of it, and you should expect to build the
bridge yourself in the briefing. That is a finding, not a failure of searching —
see **Gaps**.

## Recommendations

### 1. Danielle Resnick — Nonresident Fellow, Global Economy and Development

**Why them.** Resnick is the only person I could verify writing on exactly your
question. Her June 2026 piece on Senegal is a case study in the interaction you
want: the Faye–Sonko rupture is driven by whether to restructure with the IMF over
roughly $13 billion in hidden debt, and she connects that fight directly to
security — jihadist blockades out of Mali on Senegal's eastern border cutting
exports by about a quarter, compounding the fiscal squeeze that made the IMF
program necessary. That is the causal chain your team needs, worked through a live
case. Her comparative frame is political economy of governance rather than
sovereign finance, which means she talks about restructuring as a domestic
political survival problem — usually the part briefings get wrong.

**Recent work.**
- [A new schism in Senegal marks uncharted territory for Africa's democratic beacon](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/a-new-schism-in-senegal-marks-uncharted-territory-for-africas-democratic-beacon/) — June 9, 2026
- [Rethinking strategies for a peaceful, secure, and resilient Africa](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/rethinking-strategies-for-a-peaceful-secure-and-resilient-africa/) (with Landry Signé), Foresight Africa 2026 Chapter 4 — January 14, 2026 (covers Mali's four coups since 2011; institutional, not fiscal)
- [Prospects for democratic resilience in Africa during uncertain times](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/PROSPECTS-FOR-DEMOCRATIC-RESILIENCE-IN-AFRICA-DURING-UNCERTAIN-TIMES.pdf) (with Signé) — September 2025

**Background.** Her Brookings title is nonresident fellow, Global Economy and
Development; her day job is senior research fellow at IFPRI. Her stated research
areas are political economy of agricultural policy and food systems,
decentralization, urban governance, informality, and democratization — note that
sovereign debt is not among them, so she is strong on the politics of the talks and
should not be your source on restructuring mechanics.

**Access.** Nonresident, mid-career — on this roster that combination is usually
the fastest reply. Two routing notes: her institutional home is IFPRI, so her press
office may not be Brookings', and mid-career nonresidents typically respond to
direct email faster than they clear a Brookings scheduling request. Best single
call if you want one conversation.

### 2. Vera Songwe — Nonresident Senior Fellow, Global Economy and Development, Africa Growth Initiative

**Why them.** Songwe is the restructuring half of the question at the highest level
available. Her February 2026 argument is specifically about *timing* — that
negotiations dragging on for years leave countries exposed to exactly the kind of
external shock a security crisis delivers, and that creditors need to separate
liquidity shortfalls from genuine solvency problems and surface hidden debt before
it compounds. That framing maps cleanly onto the Sahel, where the shock is
security. She is also the person who can tell your team how the creditor room
actually behaves, which no one else on this list can.

**Recent work.**
- [Timing Africa's debt restructuring](https://www.project-syndicate.org/commentary/ethiopia-shows-how-debt-restructuring-delays-allow-crises-to-escalate-by-vera-songwe-2026-02) — Project Syndicate, February 2026. *Caveat: project-syndicate.org is blocked from this session, so I confirmed title, author, date and argument from two independent secondary sources ([Business & Financial Times](https://thebftonline.com/2026/02/09/timing-africas-debt-restructuring/), February 9, 2026; [African Newspage](https://www.africannewspage.net/2026/02/op-ed-timing-africas-debt-restructuring-by-vera-songwe/)) rather than the original. Verify before quoting.*
- [Unlocking Africa's critical minerals for broad-based prosperity and global competitiveness](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/unlocking-africas-critical-minerals-for-broad-based-prosperity-and-global-competitiveness/) (with Ede Ijjasz-Vasquez and Landry Signé), Foresight Africa 2026 — 2026
- [Key takeaways from the Brookings conversation featuring the UN Expert Group on Debt](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/key-takeaways-brookings-un-expert-group-on-debt/) — April 29, 2025 (panelist)

**Background.** Former U.N. Under-Secretary-General and Executive Secretary of the
U.N. Economic Commission for Africa; founder and chair of the Liquidity and
Sustainability Facility; co-chair of the Expert Review on Debt, Nature and Climate.
Interests listed as fiscal policy, innovative development financing, energy and
economic governance.

**Access.** Hardest calendar on this list, and the one worth spending effort on if
your program team can absorb a shorter, higher-altitude session. Route through
Brookings Global Economy and Development comms; expect lead time measured in weeks
rather than days. Flag for your team: her LSF chairmanship is an institutional
position on African liquidity instruments, so she is not a neutral party on
liquidity-versus-solvency framing — that is worth knowing, not disqualifying.

### 3. Alexander Noyes — Fellow, Foreign Policy, Strobe Talbott Center for Security, Strategy, and Technology

**Why them.** Noyes is the security half, and he is the most current Sahel voice at
Brookings by a wide margin. He is also the right *kind* of security analyst for a
debt briefing: his running argument is about defense and security *governance* —
that Western-backed elite units create coup risk — which is the channel through
which insecurity becomes a fiscal and creditor-confidence problem rather than just
a body count. He carries Mali, Nigeria, Somalia, Sudan and West Africa on the
Brookings roster, and co-directs work under the Africa Security Initiative.

**Recent work.**
- [Why Mali is at a dangerous crossroads](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/online-exclusive/why-mali-is-at-a-dangerous-crossroads/) (with Chidi Blyden), Journal of Democracy — June 11, 2026, listed on the [Africa Security Initiative](https://www.brookings.edu/projects/africa-security-initiative/) page
- [African coups and the elite-forces trap](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/online-exclusive/african-coups-and-the-elite-forces-trap/) (with Calin Trenkov-Wermuth), Journal of Democracy — February 5, 2026 (built around the December 7, 2025 failed coup in Benin and the spread of the pattern into coastal West Africa)
- [Stopping Zimbabwe's newest power grab](https://www.brookings.edu/projects/africa-security-initiative/), Journal of Democracy — July 1, 2026

**Background.** Served 2021–2023 in the Office of the Secretary of Defense for
Policy as senior advisor and then deputy director, evaluating strategic outcomes
across DoD's security cooperation portfolio and overseeing institutional
capacity-building programs in over 100 countries ([CV](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/FP-20241115-noyes-cv.pdf)).
Also a visiting scholar at the University of Pennsylvania.

**Access.** Resident, mid-career, publishing roughly monthly and quoted in the
trade press on U.S. policy toward the AES juntas — reachable and used to briefing.
Straightforward through Brookings Foreign Policy comms. Caveat for your framing:
I found no work by him on debt or public finance, so pair him with #1 or #2 rather
than asking him to carry the fiscal side.

### 4. Brahima Sangafowa Coulibaly — Vice President and Director, Global Economy and Development; Edward M. Bernstein Scholar

**Why them.** If the briefing needs one person who can hold the whole continental
fiscal picture and place the Sahel inside it, this is the name. He opened Foresight
Africa 2026 with the framing numbers your team will want on a slide — more than 50%
of low-income African countries in or at high risk of debt distress, annual regional
debt service above $101 billion crowding out health and education, and external
borrowing constrained by high global rates. He is also Ivorian and previously ran
the Africa Growth Initiative, so West Africa is not a foreign file to him.

**Recent work.**
- [Mobilizing Africa's resources for development](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/mobilizing-africas-resources-for-development/) (with Wafa Abedin), Foresight Africa 2026 Chapter 1 — January 14, 2026
- [A look back at 2025 — and what's in store for 2026 — from the Global Economy and Development program](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/a-look-back-at-2025-and-whats-in-store-for-2026-from-the-global-economy-and-development-program/)
- Profile: [brookings.edu/people/brahima-coulibaly](https://www.brookings.edu/people/brahima-coulibaly/)

**Background.** Joined Brookings from the Federal Reserve Board, where he was chief
economist and head of the emerging markets and developing economies group — directly
relevant to how creditors and rating agencies read Sahel risk. Former director of
the Africa Growth Initiative.

**Access.** Program vice president and director: the hardest booking on this list
after Songwe, routed through Brookings comms, and realistically a 30-minute framing
session rather than a working conversation. Worth it only if the briefing needs
institutional weight. **Note:** he does not appear anywhere in the bundled Brookings
expert index used to route this memo, despite the index being built today — so treat
that index as incomplete for leadership, and do not read his absence as a signal
about currency.

### 5. Landry Signé — Senior Fellow, Global Economy and Development, Africa Growth Initiative

**Why them.** Signé is the connector on this list — he works both the fiscal and the
fragility sides, and he has repeatedly convened the exact conversation you are
trying to hold. His April 2026 Spring Meetings session with the IMF and World Bank
executive directors for the Africa Group surfaced the specific institutional fact
your briefing probably needs: the World Bank is moving to a single joint Country
Partnership Framework for four Sahel countries precisely because security and
humanitarian pressure has made country-by-country long-term planning unworkable, and
the panel put the tradeoff plainly as stabilizing economies versus investing in the
future. He co-authors the security-and-resilience chapter with Resnick, so a joint
conversation with #1 is efficient.

**Recent work.**
- [Africa's top priorities for the 2026 World Bank and IMF Spring Meetings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/africas-top-priorities-for-the-2026-world-bank-and-imf-spring-meetings/) (host; with IMF Executive Director Regis N'Sonde and World Bank Executive Director Harold Tavares) — April 8, 2026
- [Rethinking strategies for a peaceful, secure, and resilient Africa](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/rethinking-strategies-for-a-peaceful-secure-and-resilient-africa/) (with Danielle Resnick), Foresight Africa 2026 Chapter 4 — January 14, 2026
- [Foresight Africa at the 2026 Spring Meetings: African monetary policy in the new "polycrisis"](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/foresight-africa-at-the-2026-spring-meetings-african-monetary-policy-in-the-new-polycrisis/) — 2026

**Background.** Senior fellow in Global Economy and Development and the Africa
Growth Initiative; research spans global political economy, fragility, state
capacity and policy implementation, and African political economy. Co-authored
earlier Brookings work with Ahmadou Aly Mbaye on the climate–development–conflict
fragility nexus in the Sahel.

**Access.** Resident senior fellow, visible and frequently convening — moderate
difficulty, standard Brookings Global Economy and Development routing. Note his
recent output skews toward hosting and framing rather than original Sahel research,
so he is a better opening or closing voice for the briefing than a deep-dive source.

## Why this order

Resnick leads because she is the only name where the verified evidence is the
intersection itself rather than one side of it — a June 2026 piece that runs from
jihadist blockades to export losses to an IMF restructuring fight inside a
governing coalition. Songwe and Noyes are ranked second and third because each is
excellent on exactly half your question and neither publishes on the other half;
between them, Songwe places higher because a program briefing on restructuring can
tolerate the security context being thin more easily than the reverse. Coulibaly and
Signé are institutional and framing picks: they will give your team the numbers and
the multilateral state of play but not new analysis of the interaction. The tradeoff
if you go down the list is that you trade specificity for altitude — and the
tradeoff if you take only #1 is that you get the politics of restructuring without
anyone who has sat on the creditor side.

## Gaps

- **Nobody verified on WAEMU/BCEAO mechanics.** This is the important one. Mali,
  Burkina Faso and Niger finance themselves mainly through the regional franc-zone
  market and regional institutions rather than Eurobonds, which means "restructuring
  talks" in the Sahel are not the Ghana/Zambia/Ethiopia Common Framework story that
  every name on this list writes about. I found no Brookings scholar with verified
  work on the regional market, monetary-union constraints, or the financial
  consequences of the AES exit from ECOWAS. Your team should assume this angle needs
  an outside source.
- **Chad's Common Framework case** — the one actual Sahel precedent — did not surface
  in any search. No Brookings coverage found.
- **The security-to-creditor-pricing channel** (how blockades, coups and junta
  transitions actually move risk premia and rating decisions) is unaddressed. The
  Foresight Africa 2026 viewpoint nearest to it,
  [Sovereign credit ratings and external debt in Africa](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/sovereign-credit-ratings-and-external-debt-in-africa/)
  (March 5, 2026), is by Raymond Gilpin, who is not on the Brookings roster.
- **A co-author worth knowing about:** Chidi Blyden co-wrote the June 2026 Mali piece
  with Noyes but does not appear in the Brookings index; if the Mali material is the
  core of your briefing, worth asking Noyes who else should be in the room.
- **Two verification caveats.** Songwe's Project Syndicate piece is confirmed only
  through secondary sources (see above). And one search result in this session
  attributed a February 2024 IMF-authored paper on sub-Saharan fiscal challenges to
  Landry Signé; on fetching the page the authors are Comelli, David, Eyraud, Kovacs,
  Montoya and Sode, and the date is February 27, 2024. It is excluded here — flagging
  it because that misattribution is circulating in search results.
